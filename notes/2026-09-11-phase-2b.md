## The finding this session turned on

**Forty phrasings per event was the wrong target, and the reason is not model
capability.**

There are not forty true things to say about "an application came to the
foreground". There are roughly twenty. A model asked for forty writes the
twenty and then invents, because it was asked for forty. Verbatim, from
`gemma2:9b` at the real settings, all of which passed a word-count and
punctuation check:

> "Scrolling through Brave's tabs."
> "The cursor blinks in Brave's text field."
> "Still waiting on the build."      — for a build that had **failed**
> "The build timed out at forty seconds."  — for a build that had **succeeded**
> "Network connection stable."        — for the toggle-on confirmation
> "Check the build log."              — an instruction, from a system whose
>                                        persona.md says it never offers help
> "Code stalled." / "Something requires input." / "Answer needed there."
>                                     — nine of sixteen `claude_blocked` lines
>                                        never said the word *Claude*

The third and fourth are the "outcomes reported backwards" failure that
[04-build-plan.md](../docs/04-build-plan.md) names in its acceptance criteria,
and the last group quietly destroys the most valuable utterance in the project.

Two responses, both kept:

1. **Per-moment targets.** `phrasebank-spec.json` sets what each moment can
   honestly carry — 24 for a command failing, 12 for a workspace switch, 10 for
   the toggle confirmations. `config.json`'s `variants_per_event` is now only
   the fallback. Full reasoning in [08](../docs/08-personality-and-config.md).
2. **A validator that rejects roughly half of what the model writes**, with
   every rejection logged and its reason counted. The rules that earned their
   place, in the order they were added, each in response to real output:
   word ceiling, required placeholders, unspeakable characters, banned
   vocabulary, **unknowable vocabulary**, **imperative openers**, **required
   anchor words**, **polarity words**, near-duplication.

The two that did the most work are the last ones anybody would design up front:
*unknowable vocabulary* (a system that cannot see the screen must not say
"tabs") and *required anchors* (a `claude_blocked` line must contain the word
"Claude", or it is not the alert).

## The authoring model changed on evidence

`docs/03` named `qwen3:8b` for this job before the job existed to test against.
Head-to-head on the real authoring prompt it was clearly worse: it writes in a
register nobody speaks in ("Brave's place waits unoccupied"), invents freely,
and collapses into one sentence with the last word swapped within ten lines —

> `{app} active.` `{app} ready.` `{app} loaded.` `{app} engaged.`

`gemma2:9b` writes plain spoken English on the same prompt. Through the same
validator, on the same event: **43 % of gemma2's candidates survived, against
18 % of qwen3:8b's.** It is now `llm.authoring_model`, with `qwen3:8b` kept as
`authoring_fallback_model`.

Two prompt findings from that comparison matter more than the model choice, and
both were counter-intuitive enough to be worth recording:

1. **Generate against a real value, never the placeholder.** Asked to write
   about `{app}`, both models degenerate immediately — the braces appear to
   anchor them into a template. Asked to write about "Brave", they write
   English. The value is turned back into a placeholder afterwards, and
   validated to appear exactly once.
2. **One user message, no system role.** gemma2's chat template has no system
   turn; splitting the instructions across roles visibly weakened
   constraint-following — it stopped honouring "mention the app exactly once"
   entirely, producing ten lines that mentioned Brave zero times.

## What the runtime path costs

Measured with `prometheus-phrasebank bench`, in-process, which is the path a
narrator will actually use:

| | |
|---|---|
| Bank load, once | 0.5-0.8 ms |
| Pick, p50 | **9-20 µs** across runs |
| Pick, p95 | **12-28 µs** |
| Model loaded during any of it | none |
| Sockets opened during any of it | none — asserted by a test |

Two things had to be fixed to get there, both found by measuring rather than by
reading the code. Writing the recent-use state on every pick cost 1.6 ms, about
eighty times the pick itself; state writes are now coalesced to twice a second,
with an `atexit` flush. And hashing every variant on every pick cost another
80 µs; hashes are computed once per bank now.

## The final bank

284 phrasings across 17 moments — 136 hand-written, 148 model-written — every
one of them re-validating clean under `prometheus-phrasebank verify`. The full
authoring pass took 17 minutes of GPU across 69 model calls, at a 68 % survival
rate through the validator.

Three things were caught after the bank was first written, each of which is now
a rule rather than a one-off fix:

- **The word ceiling was measured on the template, not on speech.**
  `{command} ran its course. {duration} later, it was done.` is nine words as a
  template and eleven as a sentence, because a slot value can be several words
  long. The ceiling is now applied to the rendered line using the longest value
  each slot can take — it caught six over-long phrasings immediately.
- **`verify --prune`**, so a tightened rule can clean an existing bank in a
  second rather than requiring a seventeen-minute regeneration.
- **`veto`**, so a verdict from a listening review outlives the bank it was
  made against: a line cut by ear is removed now and recorded, and the next
  regeneration cannot bring it back.
- **A plural-verb rule.** Every slot value is a singular noun phrase, so
  "{command} have crashed" and "{command} are completely broken" are always
  wrong. gemma2 produced that pattern twice across two separate runs, which is
  the threshold for making something a rule rather than vetoing it by hand.

And one genuine rendering bug, found by reading output rather than code:
`17 minutes elapsed` came out as `17 Minutes elapsed`, because the
capitalise-at-sentence-start pass was waiting for a letter and the digit had
already gone past. Fixed, with a test.

## Task 2.7 — `brief`, upgraded

`prometheus brief` now reads the journal into a **digest of plain facts** —
which applications were opened, where time actually went (dwell computed from
consecutive focus entries, anything under 25 seconds dropped as a window passed
through), workspace switches, closes — and hands that to the runtime model to
be said like a person would say it.

The model is never the source of a fact, only of the wording. If it is
unreachable, slow, or returns nothing, the digest's own template sentence is
spoken instead. A briefing was **asked for**; answering a keypress with silence
is the one outcome that isn't allowed, which is the opposite of the rule
everywhere else in this system.

```
prometheus brief --digest      the facts, as handed to the model
prometheus brief --no-llm      the plain template, no model involved
prometheus brief --print       what it would say, and which path produced it
```

## Task 2.10 — staged, not done

The bank is written and everything around it works, but **nothing is wired to
the desktop and nothing has been heard yet.** That is deliberate: docs/04's
review gate says candidate phrasings get synthesized and listened to before any
narration goes live, and docs/06 is blunt about why — text that reads fine
frequently sounds wrong.

`experiments/build-phrasebook-audio.py` stages two things:

- `by-event/` — every sampled phrasing for one moment, back to back. *Do these
  all sound like the same system? Would the twentieth still be tolerable?*
- `session/` — a plausible hour of work in the order it would happen, the
  claude events in Alba's voice as they will really be. *Would you turn this
  off?*

Hand-written and model-written lines are labelled in `INDEX.md` and alternated
in the audio, so the difference between them is heard rather than asserted.

## After the review — 2.10 closed

Nick read the index and played the session. Verdict: **the system works fine as
is.** No phrasings cut, no register change, nothing rejected. Task 2.10 is
closed and the bank is approved for use.

Two gaps surfaced by his follow-up question ("is there a bank of messages I can
go in and edit?"), both now closed:

- **`prometheus-phrasebank add`.** There was no durable way to *write* a
  phrasing. Hand-editing `phrasebank.json` works and is silently destroyed by
  the next regeneration — which fires on its own whenever `persona.md` changes.
  `add` writes into `phrasebank-spec.json` instead, validates against that
  moment's rules (with `--force`, because his ear beats the validator), clears
  any earlier veto on the same line, banks it immediately, and re-stamps the
  fingerprint so one added line doesn't trigger a needless fifteen-minute
  regeneration. It is the symmetric partner to `veto`.
- **`prometheus feedback`.** The entire tuning story depends on remembering
  what annoyed you, and nobody does a week later. It records a note plus the
  last few utterances plus the dial settings at that moment, to
  `feedback.jsonl`. Bound to a key it costs two seconds and no attention —
  which is the only way it will actually get used by someone working by voice.

[docs/10-feedback-and-tuning.md](../docs/10-feedback-and-tuning.md) is the
document those two commands exist to make true.

## The judgement that was offered, and declined

**The open question put to Nick, which he answered by approving as-is:** the hand-written seeds are
better than what the model writes, consistently, and the gap is audible. The
options are to keep the bank as it stands, to run `prometheus regenerate
--no-seeds` and judge the model unaided, or to decide the model-written half
doesn't earn its place yet and ship on seeds alone until a better local model
fits on this card. That is a judgement about sound, so it is his.
